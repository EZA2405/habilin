## Narrow Philippine fixture rules refreshed

Use the post-2018 ordinary estate-tax regime for a synthetic unmarried Filipino resident, with a source-confirmed fractional interest and no unmodeled deductions, marital shares, credits or penalties. BIR RR12-2018 section 2 sets the tax at 6% of the net estate; section 6(1) allows the PHP5,000,000 standard deduction for the citizen/resident scope. The implemented fixture uses a PHP15m property at a 50% decedent interest: PHP7.5m gross estate less PHP5m gives PHP2.5m net and PHP150,000 tax. A separate 100%-interest reference example gives PHP600,000; that is not the final demo packet. Asset valuations, fractional ownership and deduction eligibility still require appropriate evidence/review. [BIR RR12-2018](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No%2012-2018.pdf).

BIR's Form 1801 instructions ordinarily require filing within one year after death; specified approved extensions are separate. Implement a calendar-year baseline, not `death + 365 days`: `2023-03-01` maps to `2024-03-01`, and `2024-02-29` clamps to `2025-02-28`. The Supreme Court's Primetown decision applies 12 calendar months to a statutory year even across a leap year. The Feb29 clamp is an implementation inference from the calendar-period rule, not a quoted estate-tax ruling. Display the result as a baseline filing date requiring professional confirmation; holidays, special extensions/issuances and case-specific relief are outside this narrow fixture. [BIR Form1801 instructions](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/1801%20GL%20%20final_rev.pdf), [Supreme Court Primetown decision](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/assets/pdf/philrep/2007/G.R.%20NO.%20162155.pdf).

Retain separate filing, payment, eCAR and registration states; one proof does not establish the others. No new amnesty availability or penalty-rate claim was verified in this refresh. The fuller provenance/boundaries remain in [ESTATE_PH_RESEARCH.md](ESTATE_PH_RESEARCH.md).
