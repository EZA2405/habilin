## 1. End-to-end flow: simple intestate, no-debt estate with real property

Order below is the order the documents depend on each other. Steps 3–5 can run in parallel with step 2's publication period. Legal basis is in brackets.

| # | Step | What happens | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Route check | Proceed extrajudicially **only if** no will, no debts, all heirs of age (or minors represented by duly authorized judicial/legal representatives). Otherwise go to court (see 1.3). | Rule 74 §1 [S01] |
| 1 | Gather records | CTC death certificate; CTC title (TCT/OCT/CCT); CTC tax declaration (land and improvement) **at time of death**; proof of heirship (birth/marriage certificates); zonal value and assessor schedule for date of death. | BIR checklist [S09]; RR 12-2018 §5 [S05] |
| 2 | Estate TIN + heir TINs | Estate gets its own TIN, separate from decedent's. No business: TIN from the RDO where heirs will apply for eCAR. Registration data that differs from documents → BIR Form 1905. | RMC 75-2026 Q4, Q9–Q11, Q44 [S06] |
| 3 | Settlement instrument | Several heirs: **Deed of Extrajudicial Settlement** ("public instrument"). One heir: **Affidavit of Self-Adjudication**. Must be notarized (a public instrument). | Rule 74 §1 [S01] |
| 4 | Publication | "The fact of the extrajudicial settlement" published in a newspaper of general circulation **once a week for three consecutive weeks** (§1 points to §2's manner). RD asks for the **Affidavit of Publication**. | Rule 74 §§1–2 [S01]; LRA FAQ [S13] |
| 5 | Bond | Filed with the RD **simultaneously with and as a condition precedent to** filing the instrument; amount = value of the **personal property** involved, certified under oath. For a real-property-only estate the statute's measure is zero personal property; local RD practice on a nominal bond is **UNVERIFIED**. | Rule 74 §1 [S01] |
| 6 | Estate tax return (Form 1801) + payment | File within **1 year from death**, **electronically** (manual only when e-platforms are down); pay electronically or over the counter at any AAB/RCO. Pay at time of filing. CPA-certified statement if gross estate > ₱5M. | NIRC 90(B), 91(A) as amended [S02, S04]; RMC 75-2026 Q2–Q3 [S06]; BIR Procedures [S09] |
| 7 | OCS + eCAR | Submit complete documents to the RDO with jurisdiction over the **estate's TIN** (regardless of where paid). BIR issues the ONETT Computation Sheet (OCS; estate = "highly technical", 20 working days) then the **eCAR** (7 working days) after complete documents and proof of payment. Incomplete applications are not accepted; you get a Checklist of Documentary Requirements (CDR) listing what is missing. Original settlement deed is stamped on the back with eCAR number/date/approver. Fees: ₱100 certification + ₱30 DST per eCAR. | RMC 75-2026 Q4, Q6–Q8, Q13–Q16 [S06] |
| 8 | Transfer tax (LGU treasurer) | Province: ≤ 0.5% (50% of 1%) of FMV/consideration, whichever higher. Cities may exceed provincial max by up to 50% → ≤ 0.75%. Due **within 60 days from the date of execution of the deed or from the date of the decedent's death**. Pay to the treasurer of the province/city where the property is. | LGC §135(a), last para.; §151 [S11] |
| 9 | Registry of Deeds | Present: original deed, CTC latest tax declaration, owner's duplicate title (and all co-owner's copies), **eCAR**, real property tax clearance, transfer-tax receipt, Affidavit of Publication, court order if minors are involved. RD cancels old title, issues new TCT to heirs. Rule 74 §4 2-year liability stays charged on the real estate (annotated). | LRA FAQ [S13]; PD 1529 §§53, 57 [S12]; Rule 74 §4 [S01] |
| 10 | Assessor | New tax declaration in heirs' names. Assessor must require proof of transfer-tax payment before cancelling the old declaration. Other assessor documents (new TCT, eCAR) are local practice, **UNVERIFIED**. | LGC §135(b) [S11] |
| 11 | After 2 years | Petition to cancel the Rule 74 §4 annotation (LRA publishes a form). | Rule 74 §4 [S01]; LRA forms [S13] |

### 1.1 What eCAR is and certifies
- **eCAR = electronic Certificate Authorizing Registration.** For estates, RR 12-2018 §9(1) treats it as the BIR clearance "required as a condition precedent for the transfer of ownership" of registrable property; §9(9) says the eCAR "will serve as the authority to distribute" the inheritance [S05]. It certifies that the estate tax computed on the OCS for the listed properties was paid (or that no tax was due). It is **not** a title and does not prove registration happened.
- eCARs issued through the system linked to LRA's PHILARIS do **not** expire or need revalidation (RR 12-2024, per RMC 75-2026 Q17). Old **manual** CARs not yet presented to the RD are **no longer valid** and must be replaced (Q16) [S06].
- For shares/bank deposits NIRC §97 has a parallel rule: no transfer in corporate books / no deposit withdrawal without BIR certification (TRAIN replaced the deposit rule with 6% final withholding on withdrawals) [S03, S02].

### 1.2 Affidavit of self-adjudication (sole heir)
Rule 74 §1: "If there is only one heir, he may adjudicate to himself the entire estate by means of an affidavit filed in the office of the register of deeds." Same bond, publication and 2-year exposure apply [S01]. A false "sole heir" statement makes the adjudication void as to the other heirs (Rebusquillo, §5 below).

### 1.3 When the extrajudicial route is not available
| Trigger | Route | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| There is a will | Probate is mandatory (Civil Code art. 838) | prior doc PH14 |
| Estate has debts | Judicial administration (or pay debts first; Rule 74 presumes no debts if no creditor petitions within 2 years) | Rule 74 §1 [S01] |
| Heirs disagree | "Ordinary action of partition" | Rule 74 §1 [S01] |
| Minor heirs | LRA requires a **court order approving the settlement** | LRA FAQ [S13] |
| Very small estate | Summary settlement by court (statutory figure ₱10,000 gross; jurisdictional thresholds since changed, current figure **UNVERIFIED**) | Rule 74 §2 [S01] |
| An heir was excluded / didn't participate | Settlement not binding on them; they can compel judicial settlement within 2 years, and the deed can be attacked later as void as to them | Rule 74 §§1, 4 [S01]; Pedrosa, Cua (§5) |

## 2. Estate tax formula basis

### 2.1 Formula (deaths on/after 1 Jan 2018, citizen or resident)
```
Gross estate  = FMV at death of decedent's interest in all property, wherever situated   [NIRC 85, 85(A); RR 12-2018 §4]
  real property value = HIGHER of (BIR zonal value, assessor's schedule FMV)               [NIRC 88(B); RR 12-2018 §5]
Less: share of surviving spouse in conjugal/community property (after its own deductions) [NIRC 86(C); RR 12-2018 §6(9)]
Less deductions [NIRC 86(A) as amended by TRAIN]:
  (1) Standard deduction ₱5,000,000 (nonresident alien: ₱500,000)
  (2) Claims against the estate (debt instrument notarized when incurred; loans within 3 years before death need an accounting)
  (3) Claims against insolvent persons   (4) Unpaid mortgages / losses during settlement
  (5) Property previously taxed (100/80/60/40/20% for 1–5 years)   (6) Transfers for public use
  (7) Family home: current FMV, capped at ₱10,000,000
  (8) RA 4917 death benefits
Net taxable estate × 6%                                                                     [NIRC 84; RR 12-2018 §2]
```
Funeral, judicial and medical expense deductions are **gone** after TRAIN (not in the amended §86 list) [S02].

Law in force **at death** governs (RR 12-2018 §3; Civil Code art. 777: succession rights transmit at death) [S05, S16]. Older deaths use older rates and rules.

### 2.2 Family home details (frequent source of error)
- Conditions: actual residence of decedent and family at death, **certified by the Barangay Captain**; full value included in gross estate; deduction = lower of FMV or **decedent's interest** (conjugal/community or exclusive), max ₱10M (RR 12-2018 §6(7.2)) [S05].
- RMC 75-2026 Q38(b): if title is conjugal **or inherited**, only the decedent's share counts; Q40 parking slot counts if titled to decedent; Q41 address need not match death certificate [S06].
- BIR checklist note: conjugal family home ≤ ₱10M → deduction is one-half only [S09].
- Only one family home per person (Family Code art. 152 context; RR 12-2018 §6(7)) [S05, S15].

### 2.3 Deadlines, extensions, installments, penalties
| Item | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| File return | Within **1 year from death** | NIRC 90(B) as amended [S02] |
| "Year" | 12 calendar months (not 365 days) | Admin Code Book I §31 [S17]; CIR v. Primetown, G.R. 162155, 28 Aug 2007 https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2007/aug2007/gr_162155_2007.html |
| Weekend/holiday deadline | BIR moved the 14 Jun 2025 (Saturday) amnesty deadline to **16 Jun 2025** in RMC 33-2026 [S08]. A general next-business-day statute for this return was **not located**: UNVERIFIED as a rule. |
| Extension to file | ≤ 30 days, meritorious cases | NIRC 90(C) [S03]; RR 12-2018 §9(3) [S05] |
| Pay | At time of filing | NIRC 91(A) as amended by RA 11976 [S04] |
| Extension to pay | ≤ 5 years (judicial) / ≤ 2 years (extrajudicial), undue hardship; interest but no surcharge within extension; bond up to 2× tax | NIRC 91(B) [S03]; RR 12-2018 §9(5) [S05] |
| Installment | If cash insufficient: within 2 years from statutory payment date, **no civil penalty or interest**; return still due within 1 year; schedule needs BIR approval | NIRC 91(C) as amended [S02]; RR 12-2018 §9(6.1) [S05] |
| Partial disposition | Sell part of estate to pay; proportional tax per property; eCAR per property | RR 12-2018 §9(6.2) [S05] |
| CPA statement | Gross estate > ₱5M | NIRC 90(A) as amended [S02] |
| Surcharge | 25% for failure to file/pay on time; 50% for willful neglect or fraud. RA 11976 deleted the old "wrong venue" surcharge ground | NIRC 248 [S03]; RA 11976 §41 [S04] |
| Interest | **Double the BSP legal rate**. BIR's own 2026 worked example applies **12% p.a. from 1 Jan 2018** (20% before) | NIRC 249 as amended [S02]; RMC 33-2026 example [S08] |
| Compromise penalty | Added per BIR schedule (₱1,000 in BIR's example) | Form 1801 guidelines [S10]; RMC 33-2026 [S08] |
| Micro/small taxpayer relief | RA 11976 §45: 10% surcharge, 50% interest cut for micro/small taxpayers. Whether an estate qualifies is **UNVERIFIED**; assume it does not. | [S04] |

**Official penalty example (RMC 33-2026, Q2):** deficiency ₱948 → +25% surcharge ₱237 + 20% interest (Dec 2015–Dec 2017) ₱388.04 + 12% interest (Jan 2018–Jul 2025) ₱862.70 + compromise ₱1,000 = ₱3,435.74 [S08]. Use this as a test fixture for any penalty engine.

### 2.4 Filing place and channel (RA 11976 / EOPT)
NIRC 90(D) now: filed "either electronically or manually, with any authorized agent bank, Revenue District Office through Revenue Collection Officer, or authorized tax software provider" [S04]. RMC 75-2026 Q3 adds that filing **shall** be electronic; manual only when BIR platforms are unavailable [S06]. eCAR is still processed at the RDO of the estate's TIN (Q4) [S06]. The BIR page confirms electronic filing via eBIRForms or ATSPs, payment via ePayment or any AAB/RCO "regardless of the RDO" [S09]. RR 12-2018 §9(8)'s domicile-RDO venue is superseded on filing/payment, not on eCAR.

### 2.5 2026 issuances
- **RMC 75-2026** (8 Jul 2026): 46-question ONETT/eCAR FAQ. New for Habilin: electronic filing mandatory; eCAR venue = estate-TIN RDO; originals/CTCs required; incomplete applications refused with a CDR; TIN data mismatch → Form 1905; representative needs notarized SPA (consularized/apostilled if signed abroad); processing 20 + 7 working days counted from complete submission; manual CARs void if not yet registered; tax declaration **classification** controls valuation, not actual use (Q29–Q30); total renunciation of inheritance not subject to donor's tax, partial renunciation is (Q35); no Certificate of Property Holdings needed (Q43); decedent's business TIN need not be closed first (Q44–Q45) [S06].
- **RMC 104-2026** (24 Sep 2026): eBIRForms Offline 7.9.6.2 includes "system corrections" for **1801v2018**; versions 7.9.6.0/.1 stay usable except for corrected forms. Prepare 1801 in 7.9.6.2 [S07].
- **RMC 33-2026** (27 Apr 2026): amnesty availment deadline was 16 Jun 2025; prior availers may still submit settlement proof for eCAR; undeclared property found later goes under the regular estate tax with penalties [S08].

### 2.6 Estate tax amnesty status (as of 2026-10-20)
- RA 11213 §5: amnesty tax 6% of net estate at death [S18].
- RA 11956: covers deaths **on or before 31 May 2022**; availment window **15 Jun 2023 – 14 Jun 2025** [S18]. Window closed (RMC 33-2026) [S08].
- Extension to 2028: House bill approved on 2nd reading Dec 2025; Senate bills SBN 1866 (filed Feb 2026; prior doc PH25) and SB 2329 (reported July 2026) pending. **No enacted Republic Act found.** Bill-status sources other than the Senate record are news reports: **UNVERIFIED** (e.g. https://tribune.net.ph/2026/07/30/go-seeks-to-extend-estate-tax-amnesty-to-2028). Senate LIS was in maintenance on 2026-10-20.
- A 2026 death is outside every amnesty's death-date coverage, even if extended. Do not offer amnesty for the demo case.

## 3. Real-world mismatch problems and their legal fixes

**Frequency evidence: none found.** No official statistics (PSA, LRA, BIR) on how often each problem occurs were located. The ranking below uses a weaker proxy: how directly official issuances and Supreme Court cases address the problem. Treat it as a judgment, not data.

| Rank | Problem | Evidence it is real | Legal fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Heir set wrong** (heir omitted, false "sole heir", grandchild by representation missed) | Repeated SC cases: Pedrosa, Cua, Rebusquillo (§5). LRA publishes a Rule 74 §4 lien-cancellation form, reflecting the 2-year exposure [S13] | Settlement not binding on non-participants (Rule 74 §1); 2-year claim (§4); void deed attacked by excluded heir. Fix: complete heir tree from civil registry before signing; all heirs sign or court route |
| 2 | **Title still in a deceased parent's or grandparent's name** (successive estates) | Rebusquillo: father died 1964, property still declared in his name in 2001 (§5). RR 12-2018 §3 and the vanishing-deduction rule (§86(A)(5)) presuppose chained deaths [S05, S02] | Settle each estate separately, oldest death first, under the law at each death date; each needs its own 1801/eCAR. Older deaths: older rates, notice-of-death and ₱2M CPA thresholds [S10]. Property previously taxed deduction if second death within 5 years [S02] |
| 3 | **Name spelled differently** across birth/death certificates, title, TD, IDs | RA 9048/10172 exist for this; Mercadera (Marilyn vs Merlyn) (§5); RMC 75-2026 Q11 addresses BIR registration mismatches [S06] | Civil registry: RA 9048 petition at the Local Civil Registrar for clerical errors (misspelled name, place of birth, day/month of birth, sex) "visible to the eyes or obvious to the understanding", correctable by reference to other records; no change of nationality, age or status [S14]. Substantial errors → court (Rule 108). Title: errors on a certificate need a **court petition under PD 1529 §108** (no RD erasure without court order) [S12]. BIR: Form 1905 [S06]. "Affidavit of one and the same person": common practice, **no primary-source rule located (UNVERIFIED)**; acceptance is at each agency's discretion |
| 4 | **Ownership share differs from family belief** (co-owned title, conjugal/community half) | Family Code art. 116 presumes property acquired during marriage conjugal, whoever's name is on title; art. 75 default regime for post-1988 marriages is absolute community [S15]. NIRC 85(A) taxes only "the interest therein of the decedent" [S03] | Tax only decedent's share: co-owner keeps own part (Civil Code art. 493) [S16]; surviving spouse's net half deducted (NIRC 86(C)) [S02]. If decedent was widowed, the predeceased spouse's half is a **separate earlier estate** (problem 2) |
| 5 | **Tax declaration ≠ title** (area, owner, classification, improvements) | RMC 75-2026 Q29–Q31: TD classification controls valuation; ocular inspection when improvement data conflict [S06]. BIR asks for Certificate of No Improvement when none [S09] | Assessor updates TD (LGC 135(b) gate on transfer tax) [S11]. Title controls ownership; TD drives BIR valuation class. Reconcile before filing or expect OCS delay |
| 6 | **Missing documents** (no CTC title, lost owner's duplicate, no TD at time of death) | BIR refuses incomplete applications and issues a CDR listing gaps (RMC 75-2026 Q8) [S06]; RD needs owner's duplicate (PD 1529 §53) [S12] | CTCs from RD (LRA eSerbisyo; prior doc PH09), assessor, PSA/LCR. Lost owner's duplicate needs a court petition (PD 1529 §109, not re-read here: **UNVERIFIED wording**) |

## 5. Public cases for realistic synthetic scenarios

All are Supreme Court decisions on lawphil.net, read 2026-10-20.

| # | Case | Facts (2 lines) | Lesson for the agent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pedrosa v. CA**, G.R. 118680, 5 Mar 2001 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2001/mar2001/gr_118680_2001.html | In 1983 the decedent's relatives executed an extrajudicial settlement and partition that left out his legally adopted daughter; parts were then sold to third parties. Court declared the 1983 deed invalid and awarded her damages. | Heir completeness is a hard gate. A deed missing an heir is not cured by publication or later sales. |
| 2 | **Cua v. Vargas**, G.R. 156536, 31 Oct 2006 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2006/oct2006/gr_156536_2006.html | Some heirs signed and published an EJS and sold to a buyer; non-signing heirs objected. Court: publication after execution "does not constitute constructive notice to the heirs who had no knowledge or did not take part". | Publication protects creditors, not absent heirs. Never treat "published" as "all heirs bound". |
| 3 | **Rebusquillo v. Sps. Gualvez**, G.R. 204029, 4 Jun 2014 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2014/jun2014/gr_204029_2014.html | Father died 1964; in 2001 one daughter signed an Affidavit of Self-Adjudication as "sole heir" to help relatives title the land. A nephew (heir by representation) existed; affidavit and sale annulled. | Self-adjudication needs proof of a single heir. Old deaths plus grandchildren by representation = successive-estate scenario. |
| 4 | **Heirs of Teves v. CA**, G.R. 109963, 13 Oct 1999 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1999/oct1999/gr_109963_1999.html | Heirs signed an extrajudicial settlement and sale giving their shares to a sister; title issued 1972; suit filed 12 years later. Court upheld the deed and held the claims time-barred. | Signatories are bound by what they sign, even if unwise. Get informed approval per heir before signing; deadlines run from title issuance. |
| 5 | **Dizon v. CTA**, G.R. 140944, 30 Apr 2008 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2008/apr2008/gr_140944_2008.html | BIR disallowed claims against the estate because creditors later condoned/reduced them. Court nullified the deficiency assessment: claims "existing at the time of death" govern. | Value everything (assets and claims) at date of death. Do not recompute from later events. |
| 6 | **Vda. de San Agustin v. CIR**, G.R. 138485, 10 Sep 2001 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2001/sep2001/gr_138485_2001.html | Estate filed on time but delayed payment while awaiting probate-court authority. Surcharge and interest upheld (compromise penalty struck as non-consensual). | Court or family delays do not stop penalties. Request a formal extension or installment before the deadline. |
| 7 | **Estate of Vda. de Gabriel v. CIR**, G.R. 155541, 27 Jan 2004 — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2004/jan2004/gr_155541_2004.html | BIR served the assessment on a bank that had no legal relationship to the deceased or her estate. No valid assessment, collection prescribed. | Track who legally represents the estate; route BIR notices only to that representative. |
