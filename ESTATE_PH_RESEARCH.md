# Philippine estate issue: verified boundaries and product implications

Research date: 20 October 2026, Asia/Manila. Scope: Philippine evidence; Pamana is inspiration, not a specification. This is general legal/tax information for product selection, not advice for an individual estate. Product and buyer proposals below are inferences, not validated demand.

## Decision

The strongest supported problem is **post-death financial and document readiness**, not an automatic monthly penalty for failing to write a will. A product could assist a professional who repeatedly handles estates with evidence collection, inconsistent records and financial reconciliation. The sources establish real procedural dependencies; they do not establish the volume of rework, a market-standard fee, or a defensible advantage over a general agent.

## Claims checked

| Claim | Finding |
|---|---|
| Every month without an estate plan makes estate tax more expensive | Misleading as a universal rule. Estate tax accrues at death; not simply because a living person has no plan. Missing a later statutory deadline can produce penalties. Growing assets, deteriorating records and additional deaths can complicate matters, but these are contingent mechanisms, not a verified monthly cost curve. |
| Properties spend ten-plus years in PH courts | Not established as typical. Contested inheritance can involve lengthy proceedings, but no representative duration evidence was verified. Do not use “ten years” as a normal customer outcome or a forecast. |
| A will avoids court | Incorrect. Article 838 requires a will to be proved and allowed. |
| Every estate must go to court | Incorrect. Rule 74 provides an extrajudicial route under conditions. |
| A PHP15m estate usually costs PHP200–300k in manual professional work | Unverified. Treat the supplied figure as a lead for an itemized interview, not a market rate or achievable software saving. |
| Estate tax is an ongoing monthly charge | No automatic monthly estate-tax charge before death. Late-payment interest and penalties can accrue after applicable deadlines; those amounts and recurring property/administration expenses are separate from the underlying transfer tax. Current penalty rates were not established for a specific estate. |

Sources: [BIR RR12-2018](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No%2012-2018.pdf), [BIR Form1801 instructions](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/1801%20GL%20%20final_rev.pdf), [Supreme Court on Article838](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40502), [Supreme Court on Rule74](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/17/60981), [DOF description of the one-time tax](https://www.dof.gov.ph/go-greenlights-extension-of-estate-tax-amnesty-to-reducefinancial-burden-on-filipino-families-settling-obligations/).

## The PHP15m example needs assumptions

For an illustrative unmarried Filipino resident dying under the post-2018 ordinary regime: the rate is 6% of net estate. A PHP5m standard deduction applies; a qualifying family-home deduction can be as much as PHP10m. Assume all PHP15m assets belong exclusively to the decedent, no debt/other deductions, no prior tax credits or penalties:

| Asset composition | Illustrative ordinary estate tax |
|---|---:|
| PHP15m assets, none qualifies as family home | (15m − 5m) × 6% = PHP600,000 |
| PHP10m qualifying family home plus PHP5m other assets | (15m − 5m − 10m) × 6% = PHP0 |

These examples exclude professional, publication, registration and transfer charges, including the CPA-certified statement requirement described below. Zero tax does not imply zero professional or processing cost. They do not apply automatically to nonresident aliens, jointly owned/marital assets, older deaths or amnesty cases. A zero tax amount does not establish a completed transfer. [BIR RR12-2018, sections2–8](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No%2012-2018.pdf).

## Deadlines and amnesty: do not ship a stale shortcut

The Form1801 instructions ordinarily require filing within one year after death, allow specified approved relief, and describe late-filing/payment sanctions. They also require filing for registrable assets irrespective of gross value, and a CPA-certified statement above PHP5m gross estate for post-2017 deaths. Documentary requirements include death evidence, heir/decedent TINs, settlement evidence, filing/payment evidence and asset records. [BIR Form1801 instructions](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/1801%20GL%20%20final_rev.pdf).

The December2025 DOF release supports **a proposed** extension to December2028; that is not evidence of an enacted extension. [DOF](https://www.dof.gov.ph/go-greenlights-extension-of-estate-tax-amnesty-to-reducefinancial-burden-on-filipino-families-settling-obligations/).

The April27,2026 BIR circular still references June16,2025 as the amnesty-availment deadline. It distinguishes paying under amnesty from supplying settlement proof: there is no deadline for that proof for prior availers, but it is necessary for eCAR. Approved installments require the first payment by June16,2025; missed installments can forfeit amnesty treatment. No later enacted reopening was verified in this search; do not advertise amnesty availability based on a proposed bill. [RMC33-2026](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20NO.%2033-2026.pdf), [RA11956](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph//thebookshelf//showdocs/2/96664).

## Where the procedural work actually sits

1. Before death: inventory and authorized access, locate records, establish wishes with appropriate professional work.
2. After death: identify the relevant estate, assets, liabilities, persons and authority; choose and complete the proper settlement route.
3. Tax preparation/payment and its evidence.
4. eCAR and actual asset/title transfer; completing one stage does not prove the next stage finished.

For a will, probate is required under Article838. For qualifying no-will/no-debt estates, Rule74 allows division without letters of administration, with adult heirs or duly authorized representatives for minors; its filing/publication and protection requirements still matter. Do not classify “uncontested” alone as sufficient. [Article838 decision](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40502), [Rule74 decision](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/17/60981).

Land registration adds documentary dependencies: title and tax declarations, relevant tax evidence, and—for extrajudicial settlement—publication evidence; judicial settlement needs the partition order and its finality. The current LRA FAQ also flags a court order when minors are involved. LRA publishes standard settlement forms, so generating the form alone is not scarce capability. [LRA requirements](https://lra.gov.ph/frequently-asked-questions/), [LRA forms](https://lra.gov.ph/downloadable-forms/).

## Products already overlapping the obvious version

- LAW.PH advertises guided Philippine will documents at PHP699 and extrajudicial settlements at PHP899. These are advertised document prices, not full-service estate fees or proof of legal quality. [LAW.PH](https://law.ph/).
- LegaSecure advertises legacy instructions, encrypted storage, sharing and OFW-oriented authority documents, with listed PHP249/month and PHP499/month plans. Its marketing is not independent verification of security, validity or adoption. [LegaSecure](https://legasecure.com/clients).

A questionnaire, vault, checklist, reminder or PDF generator alone is consequently a weak choice under the Hermes/OpenClaw constraint.

## Smallest credible angle to test

**Hypothesis:** a case-preparation tool for small estate-settlement law/accounting practices: “Find why this estate is not ready for the next financial transfer step, with evidence for every finding.” The practice has repeated cases; a family is usually episodic. The repeat-use buyer is an inference, not a confirmed customer.

Domain function worth testing: an evidence-linked asset/owner/heir ledger, dated rule selection, deterministic amounts and cross-document consistency checks. Examples: a title remains in a deceased grandparent's name; the reported share conflicts with source ownership; family-home proof is absent; tax was paid but eCAR/registration proof is missing. Disputed heirship or ownership must route to professional review rather than be resolved by a model.

For the deadline: use a narrow, synthetic, uncomplicated post-2018 case with one property; inject one name/TIN mismatch, one missing ownership document, and a false “paid means transferred” assertion. An agent explains and requests missing evidence; code enforces the stage/amount checks. Compare against a general agent given the same inputs. Do not claim the architecture itself is a moat, or that software can remove court queues, family disagreements or required professional judgment.

Interview requirement before any fee-saving claim: obtain an itemized PHP200–300k quote, clarify planning versus settlement, tax versus fee, contested versus routine, asset count, title history, overseas signatures and which work is repetitive. No interviews or private-case documents were obtained in this research.
